**KEY LEARNINGS**

**The first prompt :**
*"Hey, make me a roadmap."*

And Claude did exactly that — a decent, textbook 30-day plan. Learn Python basics, do some projects, here are some YouTube links. Nothing wrong with it.
But nothing *for you* either. It was the kind of plan that works for nobody in particular because it was built for everybody in general.
Generic input, generic output. The AI had no choice but to guess.

---

**The second prompt said:**
*"Here's who I actually am — a student, with SQL and Power BI already under my belt, 1–2 hours a day, learns best through videos and projects,
and wants to become a data scientist."*

And suddenly the roadmap felt like it was written by someone who actually knew you.
It didn't waste time teaching SQL — you already know it, so it built on top of it. It picked datasets from an Indian context because that's where you are. It recommended Krish Naik and CampusX because those channels match your learning style, not just because they're popular. It mapped a direct bridge from your Power BI background into Python visualisation instead of pretending you were starting from zero.

---

The difference wasn't the AI. The AI was the same both times.

The difference was the information you walked in with. The first prompt handed Claude a blank canvas and said "surprise me." The second prompt handed it a full picture of a real person with real constraints and a real goal — and that's what got a real answer back.

Garbage in, garbage out is an old saying. The modern version is: **context in, clarity out.**
